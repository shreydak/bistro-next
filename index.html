01 / THE CHALLENGE

One order happened.
What creates the next one?

“You ordered something once. How might we create a better reason and easier path for that customer to order again?”

Business objectiveIncrease repeat ordering and order frequency.

We didn’t start by asking how to improve the reorder button. We started by asking what makes the next food decision difficult.

02 / USER & CONTEXT

WHO ARE WE DESIGNING FOR?

Returning students.
Changing occasions.

College students living in hostels or PGs who already use online food delivery.

Synthesized JTBD-style goal“When I need to order food again, help me confidently find something that fits this occasion without making me start my decision from zero.”
UrgencyHow soon?
BudgetWhat range?
CompanySolo or shared?
Repeat vs exploreFamiliar or new?
SuitabilityWhat fits now?

The same customer’s next order may differ from their previous order.

03 / RESEARCH → INSIGHT
Research note

Five simulated interviews were used as a learning exercise because real participants were unavailable. They are hypothesis-generating inputs, not validated user research.

01

Context changes relevance.

What worked previously may not fit the next occasion.

02

People may repeat outcomes, not exact carts.

Reliability, affordability or suitability can matter more than the same item.

03

Behaviour isn’t permanent preference.

A transaction shows what was chosen, not necessarily why.

04

Repeat and discovery coexist.

The same person can want familiarity now and novelty later.

05

Users seek decision confidence.

The need may be fewer doubts, not more choices.

04 / DIAGNOSIS

Past experience creates confidence.

Current context determines relevance.

Working diagnosisReturning customers have to interpret many options without enough help connecting previous experience to what matters now.
How might weUse relevant past signals to support a confident next choice while adapting to the current context?

07 / BISTRO ASSIST

But what if the customer
still can’t decide?

Time alone is not proof of struggle. In this prototype, the customer deliberately triggers a simulated struggle state after browsing, comparing and changing direction.

ASSIST 01 · OPTIONAL TRIGGER

Assistance, not interruption.

Time alone is not proof of struggle. Potential signals could include longer browsing, repeated back navigation, several item views, changing searches or no suitable familiar option.

This prototype does not implement behavioural ML. It simply simulates a possible struggle state.

⑤ Offer assistance“Keep browsing” remains visible and nothing auto-opens on a timer.

09 / VALUE + MEASUREMENT

Useful to the customer.

  • Less uncertainty
  • A more relevant starting point
  • Less unnecessary comparison
  • Freedom to repeat or explore
  • More control

Potentially useful to Bistro.

  • Higher return-session conversion
  • Stronger repeat ordering
  • Higher order frequency
  • More value from existing history
Business impact is a hypothesis, not a measured result.

SUPPORTINGReturn-session order conversionOrders per customer over time

Critical hypothesis“If Bistro helps returning customers reach a relevant choice with greater confidence and less unnecessary decision effort, they may be more likely to complete another order.”

Contextual Personalisation can reduce unnecessary decision effort for returning customers.

10 / LIMITATIONS + NEXT VALIDATION

A prototype is not proof.

  • Interviews were simulated.
  • Real-user validation is required.
  • The correct struggle-detection trigger is unknown.
  • Business impact is unproven.
  • Data feasibility needs validation.
  • Personalisation comfort and privacy should be tested.
